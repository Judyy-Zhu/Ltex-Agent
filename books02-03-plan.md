## Precise interface cards for regions and contact (designed, unverified)

- Chosen closed arc: inputs center O in cart(R,R), radius rR+, distinct circle points A,B, side sign s in {-1,1}; output a point set. Formula circle(O,r) intersect {P: s det(AB,AP)>=0}. The sign chooses one of two arcs. The center/chord determinant distinguishes the major/minor choice; a diameter has two semicircles. Immediate use III26–29 compares the chosen arcs and their chords. An endpoint pair alone does not choose an arc.
- Circular segment: same inputs and guards, output closed_disk(O,r) intersect the chosen chord half-plane. Immediate use III21 places a proper angle vertex on that segment's arc, excluding chord endpoints. Def6 is an actual region; Def8 is a rectilinear angle at a point of its arc and is separate from Def7's curvilinear boundary angle.
- Sector: use the same chosen arc, output all affine points O+t(Q-O) for Q in that arc and t in [0,1]. This radial sweep handles major/minor/semicircular choices without silently replacing a region by a radian value. Existential comprehension/parser support is still unverified; preserve the set-valued function if literal syntax requires another representation.
- Gnomon: choose a proper parallelogram with independent side vectors u,v and a diagonal cut parameter t in (0,1), with P=A+t(u+v). The two side-parallel lines through P create four actual closed parallelogram cells. A corner choice selects one of the two diagonal cells and both complementary cells; the function returns their union. This preserves the general parallelogram source definition, not just a rectangular L shape. The area-sum theorem must certify disjoint interiors; defining its scalar area as the desired difference is not a definition of the region.
- Straight-line contact: primary source relation has a proper infinite line, a circumference contact point and no point of the line in the disk's strict interior. Prove equivalence to exactly one common boundary point and then to radius perpendicularity. The earlier unique-boundary-contact draft is an alternate characterization, not sufficient source equivalence until the bridge is checked.
- Circle contact: two nonidentical positive circles meet at a boundary point without crossing their interiors. Exact-one-boundary-intersection characterization and the radius-sum/radius-difference alternatives require checked equivalence. Identical circles must be rejected explicitly, even when their centers/radii match.
- Curvilinear angle: choose the local region bounded by circle arc and tangent/chord at contact; compare germs by inclusion in a sufficiently small positive neighborhood, retaining side/orientation. III16's horn is not a positive ordinary real radian angle. Planning preserves the source meaning; implementation of this semantic interface is deferred while the user's preference question remains pending.

All cards distinguish semantic role (set-valued function or independent relation) from theorem laws and verification status. No trust/axiom is a replacement for an unimplemented region.

Historical source audit that guided the now-checked III7–8 proofs: III7–8 require point-to-circle distance extrema and reflection/equal-distance statements, beyond current perpendicular-foot-to-line interfaces. A nearest/farthest circle-point function must have P!=O in its domain; uniqueness fails at the center. Candidate radial formulas O±r(P−O)/distance(O,P) require positive denominator, actual circle membership and global extremum proofs. Also, geo.lit::circumcenter_equidistant at line807 is only equality transitivity, not a circumcenter constructor; Cramer equation lemmas and perpendicular-bisector results must be audited separately before III1 citation.
